Executive summary

What the evidence supports, what it does not, and where the time is best spent.

The short version

The source ledger lists 206 competitions across nine categories. I re-checked the entries where a mistake would cost real time, against the operators' own pages, on 2026-11-01. Of those, 8 have both a free entry route and a cash prize stated by the operator, and 7 are still open today. 176 rows remain unverified and are published blank.

Three things decide whether a competition is worth entering: is entry genuinely free, is the prize real cash that you can withdraw, and is it still open. A fourth decides whether an AI agent can help: do the rules allow it. The table publishes all four per row, and leaves the cell blank when the operator is silent.

What actually pays, ranked by usefulness

  1. Sports pick'em with published rules. ESPN NFL Pick'em ($102,000 across 58 prizes, free, 10 entries, open to 10 Jan 2027) and CBS Pro Football Pick'em (free, 50 states + DC, 18 weekly contests). Free entry and prize tables are written into the official rules, which is exactly what a serious entrant wants. Skill, not luck, decides the tie-breakers, and the volume of prizes means a competent process can cash occasionally.
  2. AI competitions. Devpost hackathons (six figures live: PayPal $67,500 closing 12 Nov 2026, Kiroween $100,000, Meta VR $1,000,000) and Kaggle (ARC Prize 2026 $2,000,000, NFL Big Data Bowl 2027 $100,000) publish real cash pools. This is the only category where an agent can do most of the work.
  3. Forecasting. Metaculus pays cash for accuracy, has 19 live tournaments on the audit date, pools from $3,400 to $50,000, is free to enter, and runs a dedicated bot track. Lowest cost per attempt of anything here.
  4. Photography and literary prizes. Sony World Photography Awards ($25,000 / $5,000, free) and the Wergle Flomp humour poetry contest ($5,100, no fee) are free and open — but the work must be human, and photography rules usually exclude generated images.

What to stop doing

  • Cash skill-game apps. Papaya, Skillz and Pocket7Games free rooms pay gems, not dollars. The ledger listed four of them as skill tournaments; all four are now marked as requiring an entry fee for real cash.
  • Tipster and affiliate "predictor" games. Andy's £1,000,000 predictor closed on 18 Sep 2026, is UK/Ireland only, and the headline is a near-impossible perfect-table jackpot on a betting-affiliate site.
  • Broker demo contests. The three demo-account contests in the market category are sourced only from affiliate blogs, not from the brokers.
  • Dead links presented as live contests. The Dirty Dozen row points at a gambling-affiliate page that now serves a generic casino lobby; Challenge.gov, cited as the official source for federal prizes, was sunset on 30 March 2026.

How the verification was done

For each entry I opened the operator's own page and recorded: the state of free entry, the prize as written, the deadline, eligibility, AI rules and payment method — each with a verbatim quote and the URL. A field is published only when the quote supports it. Where the operator is silent, the cell stays blank and the silence itself is recorded as "not stated". Where the operator contradicts the ledger, the row is flagged and the contradiction is shown.

This pass covered 34 entries in depth. The rest are inherited leads: they keep their ledger link, they are labelled unverified, and no value from them is published. That is deliberate — repeating an unverified number is how a list like this becomes wrong.

Limits you should hold me to

  • Official rules change without notice. Every row carries the date it was read; re-check before you invest hours.
  • Prize pools are not expected winnings. A $100,000 pool spread over thousands of entrants is not $100,000 of value to you, and I have not invented any win probability or hourly rate.
  • "Free entry confirmed" means the operator says no fee is required. It does not mean zero cost: your time, compute and travel are real.
  • Link health is only checked for the pages actually opened in this pass. Unopened links may be broken — they are listed as unverified for that reason.
  • Tax and payment treatment differ by country and are not legal advice.
