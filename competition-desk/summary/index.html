What we found

The original catalog contains 206 entries across nine categories. All are preserved, alongside three specific competitions found on official pages. The catalog combines platforms, individual contests, mini-leagues, non-cash games, pool tools and historical promotions. It is not a list of 206 currently open, free cash competitions.

8 rows have some independently inspected official evidence; 201 have no independently confirmed contest fields. No row is cleared on every requirement. All 209 remain on the Flagged for Review page because at least one critical field is unknown.

3 rows have explicit free-entry and cash-prize evidence. This includes the Metaculus platform directory, its specific Fall FutureEval event, and Wergle Flomp. These are not three distinct entry-ready recommendations: the platform and event overlap, and the writing contest prohibits AI-generated text.

Bottom line

Start with the FutureEval bot tournament’s rules and setup. Investigate the NFL Big Data Bowl and GEMS task requirements next, but do not assume free entry or permission to use generative AI. ARC Prize has a near-immediate submission deadline. Do not spend on deposits or compute until the entry conditions, data license and tool rules are established.

Where to spend research time

  1. FutureEval Fall 2026: $50,000 pool, bot-only, closes January 6, 2027. Strongest explicit match for AI-agent participation among pages inspected. Scored questions have short windows, and joining late may be a disadvantage. Official event · Rules & setup.
  2. NFL Big Data Bowl 2027: $100,000 total; January 6, 2027, 23:59 UTC deadline. Data analysis and a public notebook/writeup are required. Free entry, full eligibility and AI assistance remain unconfirmed. Official rules.
  3. GEMS Prize: $300,000 pool; December 3, 2026, 23:59 UTC deadline. Algorithm and geospatial data task. Entry fee, full eligibility and generative-AI permission remain unconfirmed. Official overview.
  4. ARC Prize: $2 million across three tracks; submissions November 2 and papers November 8. No API access during Kaggle evaluation; open-source requirements apply. Entry fee and full eligibility not reconfirmed. Official terms.

This order is an editorial research recommendation for the stated goal. There are no fabricated win probabilities, estimated hourly earnings, difficulty scores or guaranteed returns.

Original site audit

  • Discoverability: its landing page explicitly says “No search box, no filters.” The new directory adds both, with category subpages and saved research.
  • Reading load: long cards repeat signup, payout and terms sections for 206 entries. A compact comparison table now puts unknowns beside confirmed facts.
  • Inconsistent dates: source header says “Pass C · 17 Aug 2026”; data says reviewed August 19, pass S. Neither is treated as a fresh verification date.
  • Overbroad free count: original “True free paths” is derived from full or partial freePath labels, not independently checked current free-cash rules.
  • Tax statement: the original blanket $600+ statement is not carried over as advice. Current reporting duties require authoritative, jurisdiction-specific review.
  • Mobile: original markup was inspected, not device-tested here. The replacement is tested separately at mobile widths; it does not claim a measured usability score for the original.

How verification works

  1. Preserve each original row with its import provenance.
  2. Check linked operator pages. Network errors and HTTP failures are recorded, not called “scams” or assumed to be permanent dead links.
  3. Populate a fact only after inspecting official text. Each populated field has a URL, excerpt, check date and method.
  4. Leave unknown values empty in JSON/CSV; display an em dash marked Unverified.
  5. Flag missing source links, delegated rules, unsupported prizes, personal eligibility and AI restrictions.

HTTP 200 means a page responded; it does not verify a contest. A general platform homepage does not prove a named mini-league exists. Imported categories and names are provenance labels, not independently confirmed operator claims.

Expected-value ranking is not possible without reliable entrants, win rates, time, costs and payout data. None are invented.
